Fast settings for the moon, stars, sunrise, clouds and Taiwan’s coast. Start with the simple method; use Pro mode only when you have time.

Before every important photoClean the lens

Use the microfibre cloth, choose the 1× main camera, hold still and take several frames. A clean lens matters more than complicated settings.

Ten-second setup

Do these first

1

Use 1× and 4:3

The 1× main camera gives the best quality. Use 0.6× only in good daylight. Avoid heavy digital zoom.

2

Grid + level on

Keep the sea horizon straight and place it on the upper or lower grid line—not through the middle of every picture.

3

Protect bright details

Tap the bright sky, moon or sunlit cloud, then pull the brightness slider slightly down until detail returns.

200MP mode: use it for still scenery in strong daylight when you want to crop later. Normal Photo mode is better in low light and for quick family pictures.

Bright subject, dark sky

Moon

Fast method

Photo mode

  1. Brace the phone against a railing or wall.
  2. Use 1×, then zoom only enough to frame the moon.
  3. Tap the moon and drag exposure well down.
  4. Use the 2-second timer and take several shots.
Pro mode starting point

ISO 50–100 · 1/250 sec

Set focus near infinity and use the 2-second timer. If the moon is still a white disc, try 1/500 or 1/1000 sec. If too dark, try 1/125 sec. Do not use Night mode for a detailed moon.

Expectation: the phone has no long optical telephoto lens. Aim for a sharp moon within a wider landscape, then crop modestly rather than expecting telescope-level detail.

Stars and Alishan darkness

Night sky

No tripod

Night mode

Find a railing or solid surface, use 1×, hold completely still after pressing the shutter and let the phone finish processing. Include a tree, roofline or mountain silhouette for scale.

Tripod / firm support

ISO 800 · 10 seconds

In Pro mode use 1×, focus at infinity, white balance around 3800–4500K and a 2-second timer. Try ISO 1600 or 15 seconds only if the frame is too dark; reduce ISO if it looks grainy.

Important: turn off flash. Keep people out of a long exposure unless you want them blurred, and shield the lens from nearby lamps.

Alishan and coastal mornings

Sunrise

Best everyday setup

1× · HDR Auto · exposure −0.7

Tap the bright area beside the sun—not the dark foreground—and lower exposure until the colour returns. Take one wide 0.6× frame in good light and several 1× frames.

Composition

Add a foreground

Use a person, railing, tree or mountain edge as a silhouette. Keep the horizon straight and place the sun away from the exact centre.

Protect the camera: normal brief sunrise photos are fine, but do not leave the lens aimed at the sun for a prolonged period or use strong magnification while waiting.

Qingshui, Qixingtan and Taitung

Ocean and waves

Scenery

1× · HDR Auto · exposure −0.3

Use the grid and keep the horizon level. Include coastline, rocks or a person so the view has depth. Try 0.6× only when the daylight is strong.

Breaking waves

Motion capture or burst

Hold the shutter for a burst, or enable Motion capture. Start before the wave breaks and choose the best frame later. Keep both feet steady near windy viewpoints.

Sea of clouds and dramatic skies

Clouds

Keep the texture

1× · HDR Auto · exposure −0.3 to −0.7

Tap the brightest cloud and lower exposure until its folds and edges are visible. Take both a wide scene and a tighter picture of the best light.

After shooting

Edit lightly

Straighten first, lower highlights a little, lift shadows gently and add only modest warmth or contrast. Avoid replacing the real sky—the Taiwan light is the memory.

Golden rule: take the safe automatic photo first. Experiment with Pro mode only after you already have one good picture.
